**My bet: sell a compiler with an accountant, not a scanner with an AI.** Other teams can show a convincing screen full of fields. Show why a field exists, why another one is *not* there, which branch a citizen will actually see, and what the machine declined to decide. That is a much better answer to “here is a new file” than a beautiful conversion of the sports application.

I would make one important product decision now: **the conversion endpoint returns the partner object, not your envelope around the partner object.** Evidence, diagnostics, proposed dictionaries and review decisions are sidecars available in review mode. No `confidence`, source coordinates or invented component types leak into the import file. Stateless API mode need not retain even the sidecars.

## 3. Ten defence questions

1. **“Have you actually imported these exports?”** Schema validity and our renderer do not establish importability. Our release record distinguishes tested-in-generator, consistent-with-reference and untested features; without access we claim only the latter two.
2. **“Why did your one-page PDF become four tabs?”** The paper supplies the matter, while the service profile contributes reusable applicant, representation, contact and finish structure. Toggle the origins and inspect the evidence for each non-standard field and the applicability decision for each standard block.
3. **“What stops a company being asked for the citizen's PESEL?”** We distinguish the authenticated human, the party on whose behalf they act, and their capacity; a field belongs to a role, not merely a page. Route fixtures check which role's fields are shown and which defaults are locked.
4. **“Which source wins when the schema and both examples disagree?”** We choose per disputed path, preserve the evidence and pin the choice to a compatibility profile. Our linter distinguishes a normative violation from an observed convention, and a real importer test would outrank speculation about its implementation.
5. **“What's the accuracy on a file you've never seen?”** We have no defensible single accuracy number from seven known forms or 661 unlabelled files. We can show separately what was detected, mapped, omitted, left unresolved and manually verified on held-out pages.
6. **“Could you silently change a legal declaration?”** We preserve source wording and attach its location; OCR text remains a transcription, not an authenticated original. Ambiguous declarations are review blockers, and plain-language suggestions are separate from legally operative text.
7. **“How do you handle the six-page waste calculation?”** We distinguish capturing operands and units from implementing an enforceable formula. If the target profile does not demonstrate a calculation mechanism, we flag the missing behaviour rather than export an invented key or pretend a helper computes tax.
8. **“What happens if a parser hangs during a batch?”** Our intended failure boundary is one bounded job with cleanup and a stage-specific error, not a wedged API or a partially successful JSON response. The acceptance test is an over-budget job immediately followed by a successful normal job, and we report whether that test passed on the submitted image.
9. **“What is the national rollout cost?”** We measure native and scanned documents separately on named hardware, then price CPU time and reviewer time with explicit volume assumptions. We do not multiply a best-case PDF latency by 2,477 and call it a business case.
10. **“Why not just use a local vision-language model?”** Candidate detection is not the same problem as versioned, testable composition into your proprietary service grammar. The default needs no weights beyond any OCR language data, and a future local recogniser could propose brief facts without gaining authority to emit partner JSON.
